Design notes

  • Scored without a variable. PowerPoint can't hold a running total, so the score is carried by position — each decision branches to a different copy of the next slide, and where you land encodes what you've earned.
  • Three parts. Parts 1–2 are scored and converging; Part 3 branches genuinely by exit choice, resolving to one of five endings.
  • Built on the Toxic Triangle model (Padilla, Hogan & Kaiser) — destructive leader, susceptible followers, conducive environment — with Karpman's Rescuer role for the learner's own part in it.

Research

D. T. Suzuki described Zen as "looking into the nature of one's own being" — an emphasis on direct awareness that sits in sharp contrast to the fragmented attention of current adolescent media environments. Zen-informed pacing was used deliberately here to:

  • Train sustained attention — focus on one object or task, reducing the cognitive switching cost of rapid digital stimuli.
  • Interrupt automatic behaviour — build awareness of impulse before action.
  • Improve tolerance of stillness — comfort with silence and slower pacing.
  • Support metacognition — noticing when attention has drifted, and returning it deliberately.

Observations in the classroom

  • Memorisation alone wasn't durable. Students relying on word lists showed weaker long-term retention than those working with language in context.
  • Word origins strengthened independence. Students introduced to Greek and Latin roots could infer unfamiliar words, reducing reliance on memorisation.
  • Multimodal input deepened encoding. Music, visuals, games and interactive tasks outperformed single-mode instruction.
  • Contextual repetition beat rote review. Concepts revisited in varied contexts were retained better than isolated revision.
Articulate 360 · Course

Learning English through Greek & Latin Roots

A major obstacle in English language learning is the expectation that students memorise thousands of individual words — producing heavy cognitive load and poor retention, particularly for adolescents already managing competing academic demands.

Rather than treating English as disconnected vocabulary, students are guided to see how it is built from recurring roots, prefixes and suffixes, so they can work out unfamiliar language themselves. Autonomy replaces memorisation.
